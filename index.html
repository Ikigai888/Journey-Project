The part no one prepares you for

Everyone hands you resume tips and job boards. No one tells you what to do with the Tuesday morning when there's nowhere to be, or the quiet question of who you are without the role you had yesterday.

The research is blunt about it: most people don't struggle because they can't eventually find work — they struggle in the months in between. Six in ten people don't tell anyone how hard it hit. That silence is the problem Journey is built for.

A path, not a pep talk

Journey is a structured path with three phases. You always know where you are and what's next — and you stay as long as it's useful, not for a fixed run.

You can get advice for free. That's not what's missing.

Reddit, Blind, and the layoff communities are genuinely good. Post at 3am and people will show up for you with real empathy and real advice — that matters, and it helps.

But nobody there checks on you next Tuesday. No one notices when you go quiet for two weeks, or remembers what you were working through last month, or hands you the right thing at the right time instead of whatever happened to get posted that day.

Journey isn't more advice. It's continuity — someone holding the thread of your ninety days when you can't.

Why I'm building this

I'm Tad Natsuhara, a senior product designer. I've been laid off twice in eighteen months — once in a restructuring last year, and again in June.

The first time took five months. I had the network, the resume, and fifteen years of experience, and I still spent the first two of those months not knowing who I was without the job. I got through it. I also did it badly, and almost entirely alone.

I'm in it again now. This time I'm building the structure I wish I'd had the first time — in the open, while I'm still living it.

I'm not a therapist or a career coach. I'm a designer who has been through this twice and is building something I need as much as you do.
