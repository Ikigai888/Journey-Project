The part no one prepares you for

Everyone hands you resume tips and job boards. Nobody mentions the Tuesday morning when there's nowhere to be, or the quiet question of who you are without yesterday's job title.

Most people who struggle after a layoff aren't struggling because they can't eventually find work. They struggle in the months in between. Six in ten tell no one how hard it hit. In the interviews behind this program, nearly everyone brought up the isolation without being asked. That silence is what Journey is for.

Three phases, at your pace

You always know where you are and what comes next. You stay as long as it's useful, and there is no fixed end date.

Reground

Steady the ground under you. Make sense of what happened, rebuild a routine, and hold off on big decisions until you're on firmer footing.

Reorient

Work out who you're becoming and which directions are worth testing. You leave with one you believe in.

Rebuild

Move with momentum. A plan you're actually running, someone keeping you honest, and a clear finish into your next chapter.

Free advice is easy to find

Reddit, Blind and the layoff communities are good. Post at 3am and people show up with real empathy and real advice.

What they can't do is check on you next Tuesday. Nobody notices when you go quiet for two weeks, remembers what you were working through last month, or hands you the right thing at the right moment.

Journey gives you continuity: one person holding the thread, week after week, when you can't.

Why I'm building this

Two layoffs in eighteen months. The second was in June.

The first search took five months. I had the network, the resume and fifteen years of experience, and I still spent two of those months not knowing who I was without the job.

I got through it, but badly and almost entirely alone.

I'm in it again. This time I'm not doing it alone, and you don't have to either.

I'm not a therapist or a career coach. I'm Tad Natsuhara, a designer who has been through this twice and is building what I couldn't find.
